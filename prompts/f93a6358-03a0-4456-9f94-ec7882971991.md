## English

Turn project or team material into evidence-based, actionable rules that can be checked in practice.

[View on Reset / 在网站查看](https://reset.skill2web.com/en/prompts/community/f93a6358-03a0-4456-9f94-ec7882971991/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=useful_prompt&utm_content=f93a6358_03a0_4456_9f94_ec7882971991_en)

**Tool / 工具:** 通用

**Input / 输入:** Pasted code, documentation, standards, or process notes; alternatively, files that are actually accessible.

**Expected result / 预期结果:** Prioritized rules with supporting evidence, rationale, examples, and verification guidance, plus a quick reference, checklist, and unresolved questions.

**Audience / 适用对象:** Developers, maintainers, and team leads who need clear, reusable project standards.

**When to use / 使用时机:** Turn project or team material into evidence-based, actionable rules that can be checked in practice.

**Completion / 完成标准:** Prioritized rules with supporting evidence, rationale, examples, and verification guidance, plus a quick reference, checklist, and unresolved questions.

**Requirements / 要求:** Conversation / 对话

### Prompt / 提示词

```text
Create a practical set of project or team rules from the material provided. The material may be pasted text or accessible files such as source code, documentation, standards, or process notes. Do not assume access to a repository or files unless they are actually available.

Analyze the material for explicit requirements, recurring conventions, and well-supported implicit rules. Separate evidence from inference: for each rule, cite a relevant file, section, or supplied example when possible. Do not present a guess as an established team practice. If the evidence is insufficient or contradictory, mark the rule as provisional or ask a focused question.

Select rules that prevent meaningful errors or inconsistency; avoid generic advice and rules that merely restate obvious conventions. Group the useful rules by topic and prioritize them by consequence: critical rules protect against serious failures, important rules prevent recurring friction or costly rework, and guidelines are recommended practices that allow justified exceptions.

Write each rule as a clear, actionable statement that can be checked in review or, where feasible, automatically. For each rule, include its rationale, a positive and negative example when the supplied material supports them, and a practical way to verify compliance. Do not invent project-specific examples, commands, tools, or tests. Mark suggested verification that has not actually been run as unverified.

Finish with a concise quick-reference list and a checklist derived from the rules. Keep the result focused: include only rules justified by the evidence, and call out unresolved questions or material gaps. Do not edit files or claim that a rule has been enforced unless the user explicitly asks and suitable access and tools are available.

```
